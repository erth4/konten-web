# PayLater: How to Use It Safely and Avoid Defaulting

That "pay later" button is slippery. You don't have to calculate anything, no money leaves today, and the item is already in your cart. It feels like a discount. Until the due date arrives and the bill turns out to be three times what you remembered. That's the slippery side of **PayLater**.

Shopee and similar platforms are among the names Indonesians search for steadily; "shopee" scores an interest of 14 in the Google Trends data for October 2025 to October 2026, up about 5 percent. Wherever people shop in crowds, **PayLater** sits right beside the pay button. The feature itself isn't evil. The trouble is in how it gets used.

## What PayLater Actually Is

PayLater is a facility to buy now and pay afterwards, either in one go on a set date or in instalments. At bottom it's a debt, just dressed lightly. Its ease (quick approval, no physical card, right inside the shopping app) is both the attraction and the trap.

On rules, according to news reports, the OJK (Financial Services Authority) issued regulation POJK 32/2025 on running pay-later services, in force since 15 December 2025. The service may only be run by commercial banks and finance companies, which must apply prudence and consumer protection, including openness about things like the amount and frequency of instalments. For the exact provisions, including interest and fees, read them directly on the OJK site, since news reports alone don't let me confirm them.

## Costs to Look For Before You Click

Don't stop at the line saying "instalments from Rp...". Find out:

- **Interest or service fees.** Are there any, what percentage, and is it charged monthly or per transaction?
- **Admin or registration fees.**
- **Late penalties.** How much, and charged daily or once?
- **The total you end up paying.** Add up every instalment and compare it with the price if you paid cash.
- **The tenor and due date.** Which day exactly?

"0 percent instalments" needs checking too. Sometimes the interest really is zero, but other fees apply. Work out the final total, not just the promo label.

## Why Defaulting Is Costly

One late payment can mean a penalty. Several can mean a frozen account, unpleasant collection calls, and a bad record. According to news reports, pay-later financing from finance companies is recorded in OJK's SLIK credit data, so arrears may show up in your credit history. That can affect later credit applications, such as when you need a motorbike loan or a mortgage.

Ask your provider how reporting works in your case, because policies can differ.

## Habits That Save You

**Use it only for what you've budgeted.** Something you planned to buy this month, with the money sitting in the shopping line, is fine. Something bought because "the instalment is so light" is not.

**Add up all your instalments.** One Rp150,000 instalment feels trivial. Five of them falling due in the same month is already Rp750,000. A rule of thumb often used is that total instalments of all kinds shouldn't exceed about a third of monthly income.

**Set a reminder two days before the due date.** Most late payments come from forgetting, not from being unable to pay.

**Keep enough balance on the due date,** especially if you use auto-debit. A balance one day short means a needless penalty.

**Pay off early when you can.** Where available, settling early eases the burden and may cut costs.

**Don't dig one hole to fill another.** Using a new pay-later to pay an old one is a warning sign. Stop and rework your spending.

**Don't use it for daily needs.** If you need pay-later to eat or get to work, the issue is in your cash flow, not your shopping.

## A Simple Test Before You Tap

Imagine the due date has arrived. Will you still be glad about the item, and is the money there without disturbing another line? If the answer is doubtful, wait 24 hours. Almost everything you still want after a day can still be bought. What you don't, you never needed.

## Choose a Clear Provider

- Make sure the lender is a licensed bank or finance company. Check on the OJK site.
- Watch out for offers by private message promising big limits with no conditions, asking for upfront fees, or asking for an OTP code.
- Don't give personal data to apps whose owner is unclear.

## If You've Already Piled Up

Stop taking new pay-later credit. List every bill with its amount, fees, and due date. Prioritize clearing the one with the costliest penalties. If you can't cope, contact the provider before the due date to ask about relief options. Disappearing has never made a bill go away.

Open your shopping app tonight and look at the total pay-later still running. The figure may be bigger than you think, and it's better to know now than when the bills land.

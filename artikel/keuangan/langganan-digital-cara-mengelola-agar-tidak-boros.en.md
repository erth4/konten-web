# Digital Subscriptions: How to Manage Them So You Do Not Overspend

Video, music, cloud storage, design apps, even courses: everything can be subscribed to now. In Indonesian Google Trends data over the past year, service names like YouTube, Netflix, Spotify, and Canva show up regularly in searches. The convenience is obvious, but one side often goes unnoticed: small recurring charges. **Digital subscriptions** that each feel light can add up to a big expense if left unmonitored.

This article does not quote prices for particular services, since rates differ and can change. What it covers is how to take control of your own subscriptions.

## Why Subscriptions Slip Under the Radar

- **Automatic billing.** After signing up you do nothing, so money leaves without any new decision.
- **Small amounts.** One subscription feels trivial, yet they pile up.
- **Free trials.** When the free period ends, many services switch to paid automatically unless you cancel.
- **Scattered everywhere.** Some are billed through a card, e-wallet, phone credit, or an app store, so there is no single list.

## Step 1: Build a Complete List

Start by gathering all your subscriptions. Check these places:

1. **Bank statements and credit card bills** from the past few months. Look for repeated charges.
2. **Subscription settings in your phone's app store.**
3. **E-wallet history and phone credit deductions.**
4. **Your email inbox.** Search for words like "billing", "renewal", or "receipt".
5. **Shared subscriptions with family or friends** that you split.

For each subscription, note the service name, cost, billing cycle (monthly or yearly), billing date, and payment method.

## Step 2: Add Up the Yearly Total

Sum all costs over a year, including annual subscriptions. A yearly figure is often more eye-opening than a monthly one. Compare the total with the entertainment or savings line in your budget. If the total surprises you, that is a sign you need to prune.

## Step 3: Judge the Value

For each service, answer these honestly:

- When did I last use it?
- How many times a month do I use it?
- Is there a free alternative, or one I already have?
- If I stop, what do I actually lose?

Work out the cost per use. A service that costs the same but is used every day is clearly worth more than one opened once a month. Mark rarely used subscriptions as candidates to cancel.

## Step 4: Decide With Three Categories

1. **Keep.** Used regularly with clear value.
2. **Pause.** Used seasonally, such as a viewing service you only need when a particular show is out. Stop now and sign up again when needed.
3. **Cancel.** Rarely used or replaceable.

Many people rotate streaming services: subscribe for a month or two to finish what they want to watch, stop, then move to another service.

## Step 5: Cancel Properly

Cancellation is usually done where you signed up. If you subscribed through an app store, cancel in the app store's subscription settings, not just by deleting the app, because deleting an app does not stop billing. If through the provider's website, cancel on the account page. After cancelling, keep the confirmation and check next month's statement to make sure the charges stopped.

Also note when your access ends. It usually continues until the period you already paid for runs out.

## Step 6: Prevent Uncontrolled New Subscriptions

- **Note trial end dates** in your calendar, and set a reminder two days earlier.
- **Use one payment method just for subscriptions.** For example, one account or e-wallet with a limited balance. That way all charges collect in one place and are easy to watch.
- **Turn on transaction notifications** so every charge is visible.
- **Review every three or six months** on a set date, such as payday.
- **Set a budget cap** for subscriptions, such as a certain percentage of income or of your entertainment line.

## Annual and Family Plans: Savings or Not?

Annual or family plans are often cheaper per month, but they only save money if you truly use them for the whole period and the number of users fits. Do the math first: if an annual plan locks you into a service you will drop in three months, a monthly plan may make more sense. For shared plans, agree on clear splitting rules and note the provider's terms on account sharing.

## Beware of Subscription Scams

Messages claiming to be from a service provider that ask you to "update your payment" through a link may be phishing. Open the official app or site directly rather than through a link in a message, and never share OTPs or card details.

## Conclusion

Unmanaged digital subscriptions easily become a budget leak. By listing them, adding up the yearly total, judging their value, cancelling properly, and setting reminders, you can control them without giving up services that are genuinely useful. Schedule regular reviews, and the money that used to leak can be redirected to savings or your financial goals.

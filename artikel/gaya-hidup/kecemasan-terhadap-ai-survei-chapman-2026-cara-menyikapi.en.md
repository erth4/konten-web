# AI Anxiety: What Chapman's 2026 Fear Survey Says and How to Cope

Every year, Chapman University in the United States asks adults what frightens them most. The list is usually filled with corruption, war, and illness. In the 12th edition, released on 20 October 2026, a newcomer showed up: **AI anxiety** made the top 10 for the first time. Two worries about artificial intelligence appeared at once, and neither sits far from the top.

If you feel it too, you're not alone. But before getting swept along, it's worth looking at the numbers with a cool head.

## What the Survey Found

According to Chapman's release and reports quoting it:

- **AI harming the political process** came in third, with 51.9 percent of respondents saying they were afraid.
- **AI hurting the economy** came in seventh, at 49.7 percent.
- Government corruption stayed at number one for the 11th year running (61.1 percent), followed by fear of a world war (58 percent).
- Fear of biological warfare (ninth) and terrorist attacks (tenth) also entered the top 10 this year.

The data was gathered by Chapman together with NORC at the University of Chicago, from 1,221 adults surveyed online between 7 and 21 April 2026. The sample came from a probability-based panel with a margin of error of about 3.79 percentage points.

## Three Notes Before Drawing Conclusions

**This is a US survey.** The results don't automatically apply elsewhere, Indonesia included. Worries about AI in other places may differ: more about jobs, misinformation, or scams.

**"First time" comes with a footnote.** According to Chapman, the AI questions were only added this year. So AI hasn't "climbed" or "beaten" last year's score. It was asked about for the first time and landed near the top at once. That's still news, but it isn't proof that the fear ballooned from nothing in twelve months.

**Fear isn't a forecast.** A survey measures what people worry about, not what will happen. Worries can be well-founded or exaggerated. The numbers only say that many people feel uneasy.

## One Interesting Finding, Not to Be Over-Read

Among people who use AI at least weekly, 46 percent feared it could harm the political process, compared with 55 percent among those who never use it. It's tempting to conclude "use AI, lose the fear". The authors themselves caution that this is an association, not cause and effect. People who are more relaxed about technology may simply be more willing to try it.

## Where the Anxiety Comes From

A few plausible sources, whatever the survey figures:

- **Job uncertainty.** Many wonder whether their work will change or vanish, and the honest answer is that nobody knows exactly.
- **Fake information.** Convincing images, voices, and text make it hard to tell what's real.
- **The pace of change.** Developments feel fast enough to be hard to follow.
- **Extreme narratives.** Headlines promising paradise or doom spread easily, and both are tiring.
- **A feeling of no control.** Big decisions seem to be made far from us.

## How to Handle It

There's no way to make anxiety disappear completely, but a few habits help.

1. **Separate the concrete from the speculative.** "My office has started using an AI tool for reports" is a fact you can deal with. "AI will take over everything in five years" is a guess. Give your worry time to the first kind.
2. **Limit your news intake.** Scrolling through bad AI news every night adds to restlessness without adding understanding. Pick one or two trusted sources and set a time to read them.
3. **Get to know the tools first-hand.** Trying them yourself, carefully, often turns a vague monster into a tool with real strengths and weaknesses. Remember that the link between use and fear in the survey may not be causal, so this is a way to get acquainted, not a cure.
4. **Build skills that stay useful.** Critical thinking, communication, learning new things, and checking whether information is true all pay off whichever way the technology goes.
5. **Practise healthy scepticism.** For suspicious content, ask about the source, look for confirmation elsewhere, and don't share until you're sure.
6. **Talk about it.** Colleagues, friends, or your manager may feel the same. Anxiety that gets talked about usually shrinks.
7. **Hold on to what you can control.** Your sleep, exercise, relationships, and your own skills.

## When to Get Help

If anxiety starts disturbing your sleep, concentration, or daily activity, that's a cue to speak with a professional such as a psychologist or counsellor. Look up mental health services where you live. In Indonesia, the Ministry of Health's Healing119 offers free psychological first aid by phone on 119 extension 8 or through healing119.id. It's not long-term therapy, but it can be a first step.

## One Last Note

Some things about AI may really deserve vigilance. Anxiety isn't always wrong; sometimes it's a signal to prepare. What makes the difference is what we do next: stop at the fear, or turn it into one small, concrete step.

Try this tonight: write down one worry you have about AI, then one thing you can genuinely do about it this week. If there's nothing, write that down too, and let it go.

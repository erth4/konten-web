# Stop the Wedding: Signs of an Unhealthy Relationship and What to Do

On TikTok lately, "stop the wedding" has stopped being just a movie scene where a ceremony is halted at the last second. According to IDN Times, the Ashe song of that name is widely used as the soundtrack to stories about **unhealthy relationships**: warning signs once ignored, a partner's hurtful behaviour, and the nerve it takes to walk away. Plenty of viewers watch, then go quiet, because they recognise something, either from before or from right now.

This piece isn't telling you to cancel anything. It's asking you to look carefully. The information is general and does not replace professional help.

## Why the Trend Hits Home

Stories about relationships that wear a person down are rarely told in the open. On TikTok, people suddenly find words for feelings that never had a name. That's valuable.

But a sixty-second clip can't describe a relationship. The most-shared content tends to be the most dramatic, and there's a risk that every couple with flaws ends up labelled "toxic". Arguments, misunderstandings, and disappointment are part of healthy relationships too. What separates the two isn't whether conflict exists but what the pattern looks like.

## Ordinary Trouble or a Worrying Pattern?

Three questions help sort it out:

1. **Does it keep happening?** One bad night is different from a pattern that returns every month.
2. **Is anything improving?** A healthy partner owns mistakes and changes. A worrying pattern goes in circles: anger, apology, repeat.
3. **Do you feel safe?** This matters most. Safe physically, yes, but also safe enough emotionally to speak honestly without fearing punishment.

## Signs Worth Noticing

None of these automatically means a relationship must end, but when several show up together and repeat, don't wave them away.

- **Controlling.** Checking your phone, deciding who you may see, choosing your clothes, or managing your money.
- **Belittling.** Mockery, digs, and constant criticism that leave you feeling small, especially in front of others.
- **Rewriting reality.** Every complaint is met with "you're too sensitive" or "that never happened", until you doubt your own memory.
- **Threatening.** Threats to leave, to hurt themselves, or to expose secrets so that you'll comply.
- **Possessive jealousy.** Attention that has turned into surveillance.
- **Ignoring boundaries.** Including bodily limits, privacy, and the word "no".
- **Extreme swings.** Overwhelming attention one day, cold and punishing the next.
- **Pulling you away from people close to you.** Friends and family slowly start to feel like enemies.
- **Walking on eggshells.** You spend your energy guessing a partner's mood so it won't blow up.

If there is hitting, shoving, sexual coercion, or any threat to your safety, it's no longer a question of signs. That is violence, and the section further down is for that situation.

## Questions to Ask Yourself

Try answering honestly, perhaps in writing:

- Can I be myself around my partner?
- Am I calm more often, or anxious more often?
- Is there something I hide because I fear the reaction?
- What would I say to a close friend who told me the same story?
- What worries the people who love me?

The answer to the fourth question is often the most honest one.

## Before Deciding Anything

Especially when a wedding is already planned, family pressure, money already spent, and embarrassment can make a decision feel impossible. A few sensible steps:

1. **Don't decide from one video or one fight.** Look at a pattern over months, not one night.
2. **Write down what happens.** Note what occurred, when, and its effect. Notes help you see the pattern clearly, and they're useful if you ever need evidence.
3. **Talk to someone you trust.** One close friend, a sibling, or a wise religious leader. An outside view clears up a lot.
4. **Consider counselling.** A psychologist or counsellor can help you understand the situation. Premarital counselling can also open conversations you've been avoiding. One caution: if there is violence, joint counselling can be unsafe, so discuss safety with a professional or a support organisation first.
5. **State your boundaries clearly and watch the response.** A partner who respects you will try to understand. A partner who is angry about your boundaries has told you something too.
6. **Leave room for any outcome:** postponing, continuing with conditions, or stopping. That's your right.

## If There Is Violence or Threats

You don't have to wait until it's "bad enough". Safety comes first.

- **Make a simple plan.** A safe place you could go, numbers of people you can call, copies of key documents, and a little cash.
- **Keep evidence.** Photos of injuries, screenshots of messages, recordings, and medical examination results if you have them.
- **Reach out.** The SAPA 129 complaint service from the Ministry of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection takes reports of violence against women and children, by phone on 129 or WhatsApp on 08111-129-129. Numbers can change, so check the ministry's official channels. In immediate danger, call the police on 110. Organisations such as Komnas Perempuan and LBH APIK also offer assistance.
- **Support for how you feel.** Healing119 from the Ministry of Health is a free psychological first-aid service, by phone on 119 extension 8 or through healing119.id. It's not long-term therapy, and lines can be busy, so try again or use the other options on its site.
- **On the law,** Indonesia's Domestic Violence Elimination Law and its Sexual Violence Crimes Law provide a basis for protection. For dating relationships, the legal route can differ, so ask a support organisation.

## For Friends and Family

If someone close to you opens up, listen first. Don't judge, don't push them to leave, and don't press with "I told you so". Keep in touch. A person who's become isolated needs one door that stays open.

## About Blaming Yourself

"Why didn't I see it sooner?" Many ask that after leaving. The answer: the signs usually arrive slowly, wrapped in affection, and often after a strong emotional bond has formed. Not spotting them earlier isn't your failure.

The trend will pass, but the question won't. If the song made you pause, write down three things in your relationship that you've been telling yourself to put up with. Then talk about them with someone you can trust.

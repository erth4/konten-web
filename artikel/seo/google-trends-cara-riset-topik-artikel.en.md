# Google Trends: How to Research Article Topics That Are Rising

Choosing article topics often feels like guessing. **Google Trends** cuts down the guesswork by showing what people are searching for, over time and by region. The tool is free and good enough for basic research. What you need to watch is how you read the numbers, because a misreading can steer you toward a topic that looks big but is actually small.

## What Google Trends Shows

Google Trends displays relative search interest for a keyword or topic. The numbers are an index from 0 to 100, not actual search counts. A value of 100 marks peak popularity within the chosen range and region, and 50 means half of that. Because it is an index, Google Trends is great for comparing, but it does not answer "how many people search this per month".

## Two Lists You Need to Tell Apart

When you look at related queries, there are two tabs:

1. **Top.** The queries most often searched together with your keyword. Values are relative to the most popular one, with 100 as the highest. Good for understanding stable topics.
2. **Rising.** The queries growing fastest compared with the previous period, shown as a percentage increase. According to Google's training material, terms with very large growth are marked "Breakout". Good for finding topics that just took off.

## The Trap: A Big Percentage Does Not Mean Big Interest

This is the part that misleads people most. In the data we reviewed for Indonesia (October 2025 to October 2026), the query "umingle" is listed as up 1,150%, yet its search interest is just 1. By contrast, "gemini" rose 70% with an interest of 50, and "ai" rose 40% with an interest of 69. A large percentage jump from a tiny base can look dramatic even though few people are searching.

The practical rule: read the growth percentage and the interest value together. Topics high on both are the best candidates. Topics with a high percentage but tiny interest are better watched than made into a flagship article right away.

## Steps for Topic Research With Google Trends

1. **Set the region and period.** Choose Indonesia for local readers. For seasonal topics, pick five years so annual patterns appear. For newly hot topics, pick 12 months or 90 days.
2. **Enter your core keyword** and view interest over time. Is it rising, falling, steady, or seasonal?
3. **Open related queries** (Top and Rising) to find angles you had not thought of.
4. **Compare several terms.** For example two names for the same thing, to pick the one people use more. Note that comparisons are valid only within the same chart.
5. **Check the regional breakdown** to see which provinces show the most interest, which helps if your content is local.
6. **Validate on Google.** Type the query into Google and see whether the pages that appear match the content you plan. This ties your research to search intent.

## Using Seasonal Patterns

Some topics rise at predictable times, such as rainy-season preparation, a new school year, or big shopping moments. If Trends shows a peak in a particular month, publish and update your article a few weeks earlier so it has time to get indexed and gather signals.

## Limits to Keep in Mind

- **Not absolute volume.** Use it as a compass, then complement it with other keyword research tools.
- **A sample, not all data.** Results can differ slightly between data pulls.
- **Very small keywords may not appear.** No data does not always mean nobody searches.
- **Fast trends can fade fast.** A viral topic may not last, so weigh the shelf life of your article.
- **Term ambiguity.** One word can have several meanings. Choose a "topic" rather than a "search term" when available, and check the content.

## Combining With Evergreen Content

A balanced strategy: most of your content on evergreen topics that are always searched, and a smaller share on trending topics you can act on quickly. When writing a trending topic, add sections that stay useful after the spike fades, such as basic explanations or practical steps, so the article does not go stale immediately.

## Conclusion

Google Trends is useful for finding rising topics and the best time to publish them. Remember that its numbers are relative, tell Top and Rising apart, and do not be fooled by large percentages on small bases. By combining Trends, a check of the actual search results, and an understanding of search intent, topic selection becomes more directed and less of a guess.

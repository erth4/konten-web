# How to Write Clear AI Prompts for Accurate Results

The query "ai" is among the most searched terms in Indonesia in the past year of Google Trends data, and searches for ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude keep climbing. Yet many new users find the results bland or off target. The cause is often simple: the instruction was unclear. This guide explains how to **write clear AI prompts** that give better direction, with examples you can try right away.

A prompt is the instruction or question you type to an AI assistant. Think of the AI as a capable colleague who knows nothing about your situation. The more clearly you explain the situation and what you expect, the closer the result lands to what you need.

## Why Vague Prompts Produce Vague Answers

If you type "write an article about coffee", the AI has to guess the length, style, audience, and angle. The result will be generic because the safest guess is a neutral answer. When you say who the readers are, what the text is for, and what the final form should look like, the AI no longer has to guess.

## Four Parts of a Clear Prompt

You do not need a complicated formula. These four parts cover most needs.

1. **Role or point of view.** For example, "You are an editor helping me polish a work email." A role helps set the tone.
2. **Context.** Explain the background: who you are, who the reader is, what the goal is, and which facts matter.
3. **Task.** State one main task with a firm verb: summarize, compare, rewrite, list, explain.
4. **Format and limits.** Specify the shape of the answer: bullet points, a table, short paragraphs, a word count, a tone of voice, or things to avoid.

## Before and After

A vague prompt:

> Write an email asking for a payment extension.

A clearer prompt:

> I run a small online store. Write a short, polite email to my supplier asking for a seven-day extension on an invoice because a customer's payment arrived late. Keep the tone friendly but professional, under 120 words, and end with a new payment date that I will fill in myself.

The second version gives a role, context, a task, and limits. The result is almost certainly closer to ready to use, and you save rounds of revision.

## Techniques That Help

- **Give an example.** Paste a sample of writing whose style you like and ask the AI to follow it.
- **Break big tasks apart.** Instead of asking for a full report in one go, ask for an outline first, approve it, then build it section by section.
- **Ask the AI to ask you.** Add "ask any questions you need before starting" for complex tasks.
- **Name the source.** If you paste a text, say "answer only from the text below." This reduces the risk of made-up details.
- **Ask for reasoning.** For decisions, ask the AI to state its assumptions and trade-offs so you can judge them yourself.

## Revising: A Conversation, Not a One-Shot

A perfect prompt on the first try is rare. Treat the first result as a draft. Give specific feedback, such as "the second paragraph is too long, cut it to two sentences" or "make the tone more casual." Concrete feedback works far better than "improve it."

If a conversation has grown long and answers start drifting, begin a new one with the refined prompt you have built.

## Common Mistakes

- **Too many tasks in one prompt.** The result tends to be shallow across all of them.
- **Assuming the AI knows the latest facts.** For information that changes, such as rules, prices, or schedules, verify with official sources.
- **Not checking facts.** AI can present wrong information confidently. Check figures, names, and quotes.
- **Pasting sensitive data.** Avoid passwords, customer data, or confidential documents.

## A Ready-to-Use Template

Copy and adapt this:

> Role: [who the AI is for you]. Context: [background, reader, goal]. Task: [one main task]. Format: [answer shape, length, tone]. Limits: [what must or must not appear].

Fill in only the parts that apply. For simple tasks, two or three parts are enough.

## Conclusion

Good prompting is essentially good delegation: clear about context, firm about the task, and specific about the result you want. Start with the four parts, improve through concrete feedback, and still check the output before you use it. With those habits, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or any other assistant becomes far more useful for everyday work.

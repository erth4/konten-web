# End-to-End Encryption: How It Works and WhatsApp vs Telegram

Open any WhatsApp chat and there's a small line with a padlock saying your messages are end-to-end encrypted. Most people read that as "safe, nobody can see this" and carry on typing. The sentence promises a lot less than that. So what does **end-to-end encryption** really promise?

"Wa", "whatsapp", and "wa web" sit near the top of Indonesian searches in the Google Trends data for October 2025 to October 2026, and "telegram" makes the list too. So the question matters to a lot of people: what does **end-to-end encryption** actually promise, and what doesn't it?

## The Letter and the Locked Box

Think of posting a letter. With ordinary encryption, your letter goes into a locked box, but the post office holds the key. Strangers in the street can't open it. The post office can.

With end-to-end encryption, only the sender and recipient hold the key. The post office still carries the box, but it can't open it at all.

Technically, the message is scrambled on the sender's device and only restored on the recipient's. Servers in the middle, the app's company, and anyone tapping the network see only noise. Compare that with encryption between a device and a server: eavesdroppers on the network can't read it, but the provider can still open the message on its own machines.

## What It Really Protects

Message content as it crosses the internet and passes through servers. Messages from eavesdroppers on public Wi-Fi. And messages from the provider itself, since it doesn't hold the key.

## What It Doesn't Protect (Where the Misunderstandings Live)

1. **An unlocked phone.** Whoever holds it can read everything in plain view.
2. **The recipient.** Encryption doesn't stop your friend from taking a screenshot, forwarding, or spreading your message.
3. **Chat backups.** A backup file in cloud storage isn't automatically protected the same way. Many apps offer encrypted backups as an option you have to switch on yourself.
4. **Metadata.** Who talks to whom, when, and how often is frequently still logged.
5. **Malicious links and attachments.** Encryption doesn't care whether a message is a scam.
6. **A phone already infected with spyware.**

That list is long, and most of it comes down to habits, not technology.

## WhatsApp: Encrypted From the Start

According to various sources, WhatsApp applies end-to-end encryption by default to personal chats. You don't need to enable anything. Two caveats worth knowing: first, backing up chats to cloud storage is a separate matter, and the encrypted version usually has to be switched on with a password or key. Second, messages that are reported or forwarded by a recipient are no longer covered by that encryption.

## Telegram: Two Kinds of Chat That Get Mixed Up

This is the part people misread most.

**Regular chats (cloud chats)** are encrypted between your device and Telegram's server, then stored encrypted on its servers. That isn't end-to-end, meaning the provider technically holds the means to process them. In exchange, you can open your chat history from many devices.

**Secret chats** are end-to-end encrypted, available only for two-person conversations, and tied to the device where the chat started. You have to begin one on purpose.

So a Telegram user who assumes every chat is end-to-end is almost certainly wrong, unless they always use secret chats. Some security specialists also criticize Telegram's encryption protocol as less tested than the standard its rivals use, though bear in mind that some comparisons like this are written by interested parties.

## Check Your Own Settings

- Look for an encryption notice in the chat screen or the contact info menu. Apps that apply end-to-end usually say so.
- Where it's offered, compare security codes with the person you're talking to over another channel, such as in person.
- Check backup settings: encrypted or not, and who holds the key.
- Consider disappearing messages so chats clear themselves after a while.
- Keep the app updated, since security fixes arrive through updates.

## When It Truly Matters

For everyday chatter about dinner plans, default encryption is plenty. For sensitive matters such as legal trouble, health, or money, use a channel that is clearly end-to-end encrypted, and make sure your phone is locked and your backups are safe.

In the end, a padlock on screen protects just one link in the chain. A phone lock, two-step verification, never sharing OTP codes, and distrust of links from unknown senders often count for more than any feature listed in the app.

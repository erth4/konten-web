# Proxy vs VPN: Differences, Uses, and When to Choose Each

The search "proxy vpn" rose sharply in Indonesian Google Trends data over the past year. That makes sense, because the two terms are often used interchangeably even though they work differently. Understanding **proxy vs VPN** matters so you do not pick the wrong one, especially if your goal is protecting data on public Wi-Fi or keeping your browsing private.

## What Is a Proxy

A proxy is an intermediary server. Instead of contacting a site directly, your request goes to the proxy first, and the proxy forwards it to the destination. To that site, what shows up is the proxy's IP address, not yours.

A proxy is usually configured per application, for example only in your browser or in one program. Other apps on your device keep connecting as usual. A proxy by itself generally does not encrypt traffic. If you open an https site, the encryption comes from that site, not from the proxy.

Reasonable uses of a proxy include:

- testing how a site looks from another location,
- collecting public data at limited scale in line with a site's rules,
- filtering access on an office or school network,
- keeping temporary copies (caching) to speed up access on large networks.

## What Is a VPN

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and a VPN server. Usually all of the device's traffic, not just one app's, flows through that tunnel. Anyone watching the network, such as the operator of public Wi-Fi, sees only that you are connected to a VPN server, not what you are sending.

Because it is encrypted, a VPN suits protecting data on networks you do not trust, such as Wi-Fi in a cafe, airport, or hotel. VPNs are also widely used by remote workers to reach company networks securely.

## The Main Differences

1. **Scope.** A proxy generally works for specific applications, while a VPN usually covers the whole device.
2. **Encryption.** A VPN encrypts traffic between the device and the VPN server. An ordinary proxy does not.
3. **Complexity and speed.** A proxy tends to be lighter since there is no extra encryption. A VPN can slow things slightly, depending on the server and distance.
4. **Main purpose.** A proxy redirects requests, a VPN secures the connection.

## When to Choose Which

- **Using public Wi-Fi:** choose a VPN from a trustworthy provider.
- **Reaching the office network from home:** use the VPN your company provides.
- **Testing a site from another location or routing one application:** a proxy is enough.
- **Only wanting to hide your IP address from one site:** either works, but a VPN adds protection.

## What a VPN Does Not Guarantee

VPNs are often marketed as making you fully anonymous. In reality, they do not.

- **The VPN provider can see your traffic.** You move your trust from your internet provider to the VPN provider. Pick one with a clear privacy policy and a proven reputation.
- **Logged-in accounts are still recognized.** If you sign in to a social media account, that service still knows who you are.
- **It does not protect against scams.** A VPN does not filter phishing links or harmful downloads.
- **Rules still apply.** Use it only for lawful purposes and respect the law and the terms of the sites you visit.

## Be Careful With Free Services

Plenty of free proxy lists and free VPN apps circulate. The question to ask is: how do they make money? Some sell usage data, inject ads, or do not run their servers securely. An open proxy can be used by others to read or alter the traffic passing through it. Never enter passwords, banking details, or sensitive information through a service whose owner is unclear.

## Tips for Choosing a Service

1. Read the privacy policy, especially on activity logging.
2. Choose a provider with a clear company profile and track record.
3. Download apps only from official stores or the provider's own site.
4. Update the app regularly so security holes get closed.
5. Keep using https sites, unique passwords, and two-step verification.

## Conclusion

A proxy forwards requests for specific applications, while a VPN encrypts your whole device's connection. To protect data on public networks, a trusted VPN is the more sensible choice. For limited technical needs, a proxy is enough. Whichever you pick, avoid vague free services and do not treat either as a guarantee of complete anonymity.

# Selling on TikTok: Content Ideas and How to Make Them

"Tiktok" is among the top searches in Indonesia in the Google Trends data for October 2025 to October 2026, with an interest score of 68 and growth of around 5 percent. Just below it, "download tiktok" and "tiktok download" each score about 22. People watch, save, and even download. That's an active audience, and plenty of small sellers hold back because they think they must dance well or own a studio. No wonder questions about **selling on TikTok** keep coming up.

They need neither. **Selling on TikTok** is more about clarity and honesty than polished production. A phone, window light, and a product you know inside out are enough to begin.

One warning before going on: platform features and rules change. Hold on to the principles, and check TikTok's official terms for anything involving ads, shops, or music.

## The First Three Seconds

Viewers scroll fast. Your video has a few seconds to make a thumb stop. A weak opener: "Hi guys, today I'm going to review..." A stronger one: show the result, the problem, or something that sparks curiosity straight away.

- "This wallet holds nine cards and still stays slim."
- "Why does this chilli sauce always sell out before nine o'clock?"
- Jump to the most satisfying clip, such as the box being opened or a before-and-after.

Explanations come after that.

## Ideas You Could Try This Week

1. **A quick demo.** Show the product working. A kitchenware seller cooks something; a bag seller stuffs it until it's full.
2. **Before and after.** The stain before cleaning, the room before tidying. Easy to follow even with the sound off.
3. **Behind the scenes.** How you pack orders, cut fabric, or brew coffee. People like watching a tidy process.
4. **Answer buyer questions.** Pull questions from your chat inbox and comments. "Does this fade?" can be a whole video.
5. **An honest comparison.** Size against everyday objects, or option A versus B for different needs.
6. **Customer reviews.** With their permission, show a quote or a photo of the product in use.
7. **Common mistakes.** "Three mistakes when choosing running shoes" also positions you as someone who knows the field.
8. **A small store story.** Why you started, or what you learned from your first order.

Out of these eight, choose the two that are easiest to keep up. Consistency beats perfection.

## Building a 15 to 30 Second Video

A simple pattern you can reuse:

- **Seconds 0 to 3:** the hook.
- **Seconds 3 to 20:** the substance, one message only. Don't try to explain every selling point.
- **Seconds 20 to the end:** a light call to action, such as "store link is in my profile" or "ask about sizes in the comments."

Shoot vertically, near a window for natural light, and make sure the audio is clear. On-screen text helps people watching without sound. And the step most people skip: wipe your phone's camera lens first.

## Captions, Hashtags, and Comments

Write a short caption containing words people actually search, like the product type, material, or benefit. You don't need many hashtags. A few relevant ones beat dozens pasted at random. Make time to reply to comments, especially questions. A reply can become the next video, and other viewers reading along feel looked after too.

## Music and Copyright

This trap catches business accounts often. A popular song available to personal accounts isn't necessarily cleared for commercial content. Platforms usually provide a music library meant for business accounts. Check the official terms before using a sound for promotion.

One related point: the popularity of "download tiktok" isn't permission to re-upload someone else's video as your own. Make your own. Original content is safer and easier to trust.

## Be Honest About the Product

Don't make claims you can't back up, especially for health, beauty, or food products. "Cures", "guaranteed whiter in three days", and the like can break platform rules and, more importantly, mislead buyers. A disappointed buyer leaves a bad review, and that review sticks around longer than any video.

## Measure With a Cool Head

View counts are nice to look at, but a seller needs numbers closer to money. Watch how many viewers stay to the end, how many tap through to your link or profile, and what questions land in your chat after a video goes up. One busy video doesn't necessarily bring orders. Judge the pattern after ten or twenty videos, not after one.

## What Not to Do

- Buy viewers or fake engagement. The numbers go up; the buyers don't.
- Copy other people's videos.
- Hide prices or terms behind a constant "info in DM."
- Delete critical comments without answering them.
- Quit after five videos because it isn't busy yet.

Record one video today. Pick one product, the one problem it solves, and an opening line that makes even you curious about what comes next.

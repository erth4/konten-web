# Job Interview Questions and How to Answer Them Well

Getting past the CV screen means your paperwork was convincing. The job interview is where the company checks that the person behind the CV is a real fit. Many candidates feel nervous not because they lack ability, but because they do not know what will be asked. The good news is that interview questions follow repeating patterns, so focused preparation can noticeably reduce the nerves.

## What Interviewers Are Really Judging

Behind each question, interviewers usually look for three things: can you do the work, will you do it with real commitment, and will you work well with the team. A good answer is not the longest or the most polished one. It is the one that addresses those three points with a concrete example.

## Preparing Before Interview Day

1. **Learn about the company.** Read its official website, its products or services, and recent news. Note one or two things that interest you.
2. **Re-read the job description.** Mark the three main requirements and prepare one experience story for each.
3. **Practice answers out loud.** Speaking in front of a mirror or recording yourself helps you find the rambling parts.
4. **Sort out logistics.** Confirm the schedule, the location or video link, and any documents requested. For online interviews, test your camera, microphone, and connection first.
5. **Prepare questions for the interviewer.** This shows genuine interest.

## Questions That Almost Always Appear

**"Tell me about yourself."** Do not recite your CV. Build the answer in three parts: your current role or background, two or three of the most relevant experiences, and why this position interests you. One to two minutes is enough.

**"Why do you want to work here?"** Link something specific about the company to something you value or do well. A generic answer like "your company is big" sounds like it could be used anywhere.

**"What are your strengths and weaknesses?"** Pick strengths relevant to the role and back them with examples. For weaknesses, name a real one that does not block the core of the job, then explain what you are doing to improve it.

**"Describe a time you faced a problem or conflict."** Behavioral questions like this are easiest to answer with the STAR framework: *Situation*, *Task*, *Action*, and *Result*. Spend most of your time on the action and the result.

**"Why did you leave your previous job?"** Answer honestly and neutrally. Avoid criticizing a former boss or colleagues. Point to what you are looking for next, such as a new challenge or room to grow.

**"What salary do you expect?"** Prepare a range based on market research and your own needs, rather than one rigid figure. If it has not come up yet, ask first what range is available for the position.

## Using STAR Properly

Many people answer behavioral questions with general statements such as "I work well with others". That proves nothing. Compare it with a structured answer: a deadline was tight because a colleague fell ill (situation), you were asked to finish the report (task), you split the work into small parts and asked one teammate for help (action), and the report was delivered on time (result). A story like this is easier to remember and hard to dispute.

Prepare three to five stories you can adapt to different questions: a success, a failure you learned from, teamwork, resolving a conflict, and handling pressure.

## Body Language and Delivery

- Arrive early, but not so early that you disrupt their schedule.
- Dress neatly in line with the company culture; slightly more formal is better than less.
- Keep natural eye contact and listen until the question is finished before answering.
- Speak at a calm pace. A brief pause to think beats a rushed reply.
- If you do not know the answer, admit it honestly and explain how you would find out.

## Questions Worth Asking

At the end, you will almost certainly be asked whether you have questions. Use the chance. Good examples: what a typical day looks like in this role, how success is measured in the first three months, and what the next steps and timeline are. Avoid opening with salary and leave if the interviewer has not raised them.

## After the Interview

Send a short thank-you message within a day. Mention one specific thing you discussed and restate your interest. If the promised response date has passed, it is reasonable to follow up politely once.

## Closing

A job interview is not an exam to memorize for but a conversation you can prepare for. Start by writing answers to the six questions above and preparing three STAR stories from your real experience. Practice until they sound natural rather than rehearsed. Enough preparation keeps you calmer and lets your personality and ability show clearly.

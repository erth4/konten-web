# How to Write a CV That Gets Past the First Screening: Structure, Content, and Common Mistakes

Recruiters often receive dozens or even hundreds of applications for a single role, so the time they give each CV is very short. Many companies also use automated screening systems that read your CV before a person does. A good CV, then, is not the most colorful or the longest. It is the one that is easy to read, relevant to the opening, and proves what you have actually achieved.

## The Goal of a CV: An Interview Invitation

A CV is not a complete life story. It works as a short advertisement answering one question for the recruiter: is this person worth calling? Every line should support a "yes". Information that does not serve that goal should be cut.

## A Safe, Readable Structure

1. **Name and contact details.** Full name, an active phone number, a professional email, your city, and a link to your professional profile if you have one. A full home address is usually unnecessary.
2. **A short summary.** Two or three sentences on the role you are targeting, your main experience, and your relevant strengths.
3. **Work experience.** List the most recent first. Include title, company, dates, and achievements.
4. **Education.** Institution, major, and year. For recent graduates, this section may go above experience.
5. **Skills.** Separate technical from non-technical skills, listing only those you truly have.
6. **Optional extras.** Certifications, projects, organizations, or languages, if they support the role.

Aim for one page if you have only a few years of experience, and two pages at most for more seasoned candidates.

## Write Experience as Results, Not Just Duties

The biggest difference between an ordinary CV and a strong one is how experience is written. A list of duties says what you did; results say what difference it made.

A weak example: "Responsible for customer service."

A stronger example: "Handled roughly 40 customer complaints a day and reduced repeat complaints by drafting a set of standard answers."

Use a simple formula: **action verb + what you did + result or measure**. If you have no exact figures, use an honest estimate or a concrete picture such as "sped up reporting from weekly to daily". Do not invent numbers, because an interviewer may ask about them.

## Tailor It to Each Opening

One generic CV for every job rarely works. Read the job description, mark the key terms and main requirements, then make sure your relevant experience appears in the same wording, as long as it is true. Screening systems often match keywords, and human recruiters spot a fit faster when the language is similar. You only need to adjust the summary, the order of bullet points, and the skills section, not rewrite everything.

## A Format That Screening Systems Can Read

- Use a single column with a common font at a readable size.
- Give sections clear headings such as "Work Experience" and "Education".
- Avoid complex tables, text boxes, icons, and skill ratings shown as stars or bars.
- Save as PDF unless the posting asks for another format, and use a clear file name such as "CV_YourName_Position".
- A photo is not required; follow what the posting asks.

## Common Mistakes

- **Typos and grammar errors.** Read it aloud or have someone else check.
- **An unprofessional email address.** Use one that contains your name.
- **Too much irrelevant information,** such as unrelated old jobs or personal details that are not needed.
- **Overstated skills.** List only what you can demonstrate.
- **Unexplained gaps.** If there is a long break, explain it briefly, for example caring for family, self-study, or freelance projects.
- **Excess sensitive data.** ID numbers, bank account numbers, and a full birth date do not belong on a CV.

## Closing

A good CV is a short document that is easy to scan, highlights results, and is tailored to the opening. Start by rewriting your three strongest experience points using the action, task, and result formula. Then save one base version you can adapt for each application. Small changes like these often matter more than switching to a new design.

# Power Outage in Indonesia: Check PLN Shutdowns and Report Faults

The laptop screen goes dark, the fan stops, and the whole house falls quiet. Most people's first move is to peek outside: is the neighbour in a **power outage** too? That question decides what you do next, because a fault inside your home and a fault on the PLN network are handled very differently.

"Mati lampu hari ini" (power out today) is the kind of thing people type the moment the lights drop. So here is how to check and report it properly, plus a few small habits that protect your devices and your food.

## Step One: Work Out Where the Problem Is

Before reporting anything, look at two things.

- **The surroundings.** Neighbours' lights off as well? The fault is most likely on the network.
- **Your own wiring.** When only your house is dark, check the MCB or main switch in the breaker box. Switch it back on once if it has tripped. Should it drop again straight away, don't force it. There may be a short circuit or a faulty appliance, and that calls for an electrician.

You don't always have to step outside to find out, either. PLN offers a way to check from your phone.

## Checking in PLN Mobile

According to guides published by several news outlets, the PLN Mobile app has a feature called **Cek Padam Sekitar Saya** ("Check outages around me") that shows a map of affected areas and the network status. The path goes roughly like this:

1. Log in with your registered phone number.
2. Open the Pengaduan (Complaints) menu on the home screen.
3. Choose Cek Padam Sekitar Saya.
4. Pick the customer ID or meter number you want to check.

Menus can change with app updates, so if the wording differs, look for the closest match in the complaints section.

## How to Report

Report it if the fault turns out not to be logged yet. There are two main routes:

- **In the app.** Under Pengaduan, pick the power outage category, enter your customer ID, make sure the pin on the map is right, add a short note, and send. You can usually follow the handling status in the same app.
- **By phone.** Call PLN's Call Center on 123. Per the same sources, it's free and open 24 hours.

Keep your customer ID or meter number and a clear address ready. Save any ticket number you're given so you can follow up without telling the whole story again.

## Planned Shutdowns Are a Different Matter

Not every outage arrives without warning. PLN sometimes cuts power for network maintenance, and the notice normally goes out first through app notifications or the official account of the local PLN unit. Turning on PLN Mobile notifications and following your area's unit account is a cheap and useful habit.

A caution: aggregator pages listing "today's shutdown schedule" aren't always accurate, and some appear to be recycled with empty tables. Treat the app and PLN's official accounts as the main reference.

Also remember that one outage doesn't automatically mean nationwide rolling blackouts. The cause could be maintenance, an unplanned fault, load limiting in a particular region, or a problem in your own wiring. I couldn't find confirmation of any specific region being hit today, so check directly for your address.

## While the Power Is Out

A few sensible habits:

1. **Unplug sensitive devices** such as computers, televisions, and routers. A surge when power returns can damage them.
2. **Leave one light switched on** so you know exactly when supply comes back.
3. **Keep the fridge door shut as much as you can.** While it stays closed, the cold lasts longer.
4. **Charge power banks and phones** every night, especially if your area has frequent trouble.
5. **Use a torch rather than candles** when there are small children or flammable things nearby.
6. **If you run a generator, keep it outdoors,** well away from windows and doors. Its exhaust contains carbon monoxide, which is dangerous in enclosed spaces.

## Once the Lights Return

Wait a few minutes before switching everything on at once. Check the food in the fridge and freezer: if you're unsure how long it has been without cooling, throwing it out is safer than eating it.

If outages keep recurring at the same address, write down the dates and times and attach them to your next report. A recorded pattern is easier to act on than a spoken complaint.

There's one small job you can do tonight. Open PLN Mobile, confirm your customer ID is linked, and save 123 in your phone. When the lights really do go out, you'll be glad you did.

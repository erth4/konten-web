# How to Tidy Up Gmail: Labels, Filters, and Unsubscribing

The red number on your mail icon has four digits. Inside are OTP codes from 2022, shop newsletters you've never opened, and one email with the exam timetable you actually need and can't find. If that sounds familiar, you can **tidy up Gmail** in a few short sessions, without having to become a devotee of "inbox zero".

In Indonesian Google Trends data for October 2025 to October 2026, "gmail" scores an interest of 17, up about 2 percent. Nearly everyone has an account, and nearly every account piles up junk. Menu names can differ between versions, so take the steps below as directions.

## Start With a Question: What's Your Problem?

A messy inbox is usually one of these, or a mix:

- too many promotional emails and newsletters,
- important emails drowned among unimportant ones,
- storage almost full,
- no idea how to find anything.

Each problem has a different cure.

## Search Like an Expert

Before sorting anything, learn to search. Gmail's search box accepts special commands that narrow results fast:

- `from:name@example.com` for emails from a particular sender,
- `has:attachment` for emails with attachments,
- `larger:10M` for emails bigger than 10 MB,
- `older_than:1y` for emails older than a year,
- `is:unread` for unread emails,
- `label:label-name` for emails with a given label,
- quotation marks for exact phrases, and a minus sign to exclude a word.

Combine them: `from:shop older_than:1y` shows all the old emails from one sender. From those results you can select everything and delete in one go.

## Clear Big Attachments to Save Space

Your free storage is shared among Gmail, Google Drive, and Photos, so a stuffed mailbox can eat into space for other files. The fastest way to free space is to target big emails:

1. Search `larger:10M` in the search box.
2. Open the top few emails and decide what you still need.
3. Download important attachments to other storage if necessary.
4. Delete the emails, then empty the Trash folder. Emails in Trash still use space until they're properly deleted or cleared automatically after some time.

One large email is often worth hundreds of plain text ones.

## Unsubscribe From What You Don't Read

Every time a newsletter arrives and you delete it unopened, that's your cue to unsubscribe. Many legitimate emails include an unsubscribe link at the top or bottom. Gmail also shows an unsubscribe button for some senders.

An important rule: **use the unsubscribe link only for senders you recognize.** On spam or suspicious emails, clicking an unsubscribe link can actually tell the sender your address is live. For those, use the Report spam button; don't reply and don't click anything.

Set aside one afternoon to unsubscribe from the ten newsletters you delete most often. The effect is immediate.

## Labels: Folders That Can Overlap

Unlike ordinary folders, one email in Gmail can carry several labels. A few practical uses:

- **Work:** with sub-labels per project.
- **Money:** bills, receipts, and bank notifications.
- **Shopping:** order confirmations and warranties.
- **Needs action:** emails awaiting a reply or a decision.
- **Reference:** information you may need again.

Add colours so they're easy to spot, but don't create too many labels. Five to ten that you use regularly beat fifty made once and forgotten.

## Filters: Let Gmail Do the Work

Filters handle incoming email by rules you set. To make one: open the filter options through the search box or settings, set criteria such as the sender or a word in the subject, then choose the action. Useful examples:

1. **Bank or billing notifications:** auto-label as Money and mark important.
2. **Newsletters you still want:** label them and skip the inbox, so you read them when you have time.
3. **Emails from your boss or main client:** mark important and star them.
4. **Social media notifications:** archive straight away or label them.
5. **Senders that always land in spam though they matter:** set them to never go to spam.

Start with two or three filters. Add more when you notice a pattern of email you always handle the same way.

## Use Tabs and Helper Features

- **Primary, Promotions, and Social tabs** (if switched on) separate kinds of email automatically. Check Promotions once a week so you don't miss anything important.
- **Stars and importance markers** flag what needs attention.
- **Snooze** makes an email reappear at a time you choose.
- **Schedule send** gets an email delivered at the right hour.
- **Archive, don't delete.** Archiving moves email out of the inbox without deleting it, and it stays searchable.

## A Realistic Routine

You don't need to reach an empty inbox. Just make the inbox hold only what needs action.

1. Once a day, process new email: reply, archive, delete, or snooze.
2. Once a week, take a quick look at Promotions and the Spam folder.
3. Once a month, unsubscribe from one or two senders you don't read.
4. Once a year, search for big emails and delete what isn't needed.

## Don't Forget Security

A tidy inbox is also easier to secure. Turn on two-step verification, use a unique password, and watch for phishing emails that imitate banks, couriers, or Google services. Check the sender's address closely, and don't open attachments from unknown senders. Old emails holding verification codes, password-reset links, or identity documents are best deleted.

Start with one thing tonight: type `larger:10M` in the search box and see what turns up. You might be surprised how big it is.

# Cek Bansos: How to Check Aid Recipients on the Official Kemensos Site

The question "am I registered as a social aid recipient?" always draws interest. In Indonesian Google Trends data over the past year, the query "cek bansos" is listed as rising about 60 percent. The good news is that a **cek bansos** check (checking social assistance status) can be done by yourself through the Ministry of Social Affairs' official channels, free of charge and without intermediaries. This article explains the steps, why a name might not appear, and how to avoid the scams that often accompany this topic.

A note: the site's look and the service flow can change. This article does not cover disbursement schedules or aid amounts, because that information changes and is valid only when it comes from official government announcements. Refer to the Ministry's official channels for current information.

## Two Official Ways to Check

### 1. Through the Cekbansos Website

The official site for checking recipient status is **cekbansos.kemensos.go.id**. Based on guides we found in news reports, the steps are as follows:

1. Open cekbansos.kemensos.go.id in your phone or computer browser. Make sure the address is exactly that and ends in .go.id.
2. Select the region according to your ID card: province, regency or city, district, then village.
3. Enter your full name as it appears on your ID or population record. A differently spelled name can make the data impossible to find.
4. Type the captcha code shown.
5. Click the search button, or "Cari Data".
6. The result shows the status and any aid programs linked to that name, if there are any.

### 2. Through the Cek Bansos App

The Ministry also provides a Cek Bansos app that can be downloaded from official app stores. According to the information we found, users need to register with their national ID number (NIK), family card number, and an ID card photo, then log in and open the check menu to see their status and type of aid. Registration requirements can change, so follow the instructions shown in the app. Download only from official app stores and confirm the developer matches.

The app also has a propose-and-dispute feature, explained below.

## If Your Name Does Not Appear

A missing name does not always mean something is wrong. A few possibilities:

- **Not in the recipient data.** Aid recipients are determined from data managed by the government, and not every resident is included.
- **Not meeting a particular program's criteria.** Each program has its own requirements.
- **In a category that is not eligible.** News reports mention that civil servants, military, and police personnel are not among recipients of some programs.
- **Wrong region or wrong spelling.** Recheck the region, name, and spelling against your ID.
- **Population data not yet synchronized.** Sometimes records at the civil registry need updating.

Before drawing a conclusion, repeat the search with the correct region and spelling, and try a few times at different hours if the site is busy.

## Proposing and Disputing

If you believe you qualify but are not registered, or you know of a recipient who does not qualify, the Cek Bansos app offers a propose-and-dispute feature. It lets residents nominate themselves or others who qualify, or dispute data they consider inaccurate. Verification is still carried out by the authorities, so a submission does not automatically mean approval. For direct help, ask your village or ward office and the local social affairs agency.

## Beware of Scams Posing as Social Aid

Money-aid topics are easy prey for scammers. Learn the patterns:

1. **They ask for a fee.** The official checking and registration services do not charge. Anyone asking for "administration" money should be suspected.
2. **They ask for an OTP, PIN, or password.** Official parties will never ask for your secret codes.
3. **Links from unknown messages.** Do not click links on WhatsApp, SMS, or social media claiming to distribute aid. Type the official address into your browser yourself.
4. **Look-alike site addresses.** Scammers build copycat sites with nearly identical names. Make sure the domain ends in .go.id and is spelled exactly right.
5. **Apps from outside official stores.** APK files sent through messages often carry harmful programs that steal data.
6. **Promises of guaranteed approval.** No one can guarantee that someone becomes a recipient, let alone for a fee.

If you come across a scam, report it through official complaint channels and do not spread the link.

## Protecting Your Personal Data

Even when using official channels, protect your NIK, family card number, and ID photo. Do not share them on social media or with people who claim to "help register you". Use a secure network and log out when finished on a shared device.

## Conclusion

A cek bansos check can be done for free through the official cekbansos.kemensos.go.id site or the Cek Bansos app. Enter your region and name as on your ID, and if your name does not appear, check the spelling and program requirements, use the propose-and-dispute feature, or ask your village office. Above all, ignore any offer that asks for a fee, an OTP, or a link outside official channels, because legitimate social aid is never for sale.

# Google Maps: Tips for Offline Maps, Routes, and Sharing Your Location

The signal drops on an unfamiliar road, and your phone screen freezes on a grey circle. Anyone who's been there knows the small panic that follows. **Google Maps** has a few features that avoid that situation, yet most people use only one thing: type a destination, then press start.

In Indonesian Google Trends data for October 2025 to October 2026, "maps" scores an interest of 8 with growth of about 10 percent. The app sits on almost every phone, but its most useful features often go untouched. Here are the ones most worth trying.

Menu names can differ between versions and devices. If a button mentioned here can't be found, look for a feature with a similar name in the profile menu or settings.

## Offline Maps: Download Before You Leave

This is the feature that rescues trips most often. The usual steps: open your profile menu, choose offline maps, then select the area you want to download. After that you can search for places and get directions in that area even without internet.

A few things to know:

- **Download on Wi-Fi** so your data allowance isn't drained. Map files can be big.
- **Choose an area wide enough** to cover your start, destination, and routes that may change.
- **Offline maps don't last forever.** Downloaded maps need updating now and then and can expire if left a long time.
- **No live traffic.** Offline routes don't show congestion or real-time information.
- **Features can differ** for certain travel modes. Check what's available for your trip.

A good habit: download the destination area the day before you leave, especially for places known for weak signal, like mountains, coastlines, or the countryside.

## Setting Routes More Cleverly

Many people accept the first suggested route. There are actually several choices.

1. **Add stops.** Besides the final destination, you can add stopovers. Handy when picking up a friend or stopping at a petrol station.
2. **Look at route options.** There are usually alternatives with different times and distances. Choose to suit your needs.
3. **Set preferences.** You can choose to avoid toll roads, ferries, or highways where the option is offered.
4. **Pick the right mode.** Car, motorbike, walking, cycling, or public transport give different routes and times.
5. **Check estimated departure times.** Some versions let you see estimates for a particular time.

One honest note: Maps is an aid, not a referee. If the directions send you down a road that's clearly unfit, or local signs say otherwise, follow the signs and common sense.

## Save Places and Make Lists

Saving a place makes it easy to find again. You can save restaurants you want to try, places to stay, or sights into a list. Give lists names like "Bandung Eats" or "December Trip". When you arrive in that city, just open the list. Lists can also be shared with friends travelling with you.

## Check a Place Before You Go

Before heading to a place, spend a minute:

- check opening hours and whether it's closed today,
- read recent reviews, not just the star rating,
- look at visitors' photos, not only the official ones,
- check the crowd information if it's shown,
- use street view to see what the entrance and parking look like.

Reviews vary in honesty. Read a few, look for a pattern, and discount praise or abuse that seems over the top.

## Sharing Your Location: Useful, but Set It Up Carefully

You can share your live location with specific people so they can follow your journey. Useful when heading home late, waiting for a ride, or meeting in a crowded place. The usual route is through the profile menu and the location-sharing option, then choosing a duration and recipients. Duration choices usually include a set time or until you turn it off.

Some safe habits:

1. **Share only with people you trust.**
2. **Pick the shortest duration possible.** Don't let "until turned off" run for months.
3. **Stop sharing once you've arrived.** You can switch it off any time from the same screen.
4. **Check periodically** who can still see your location.
5. **Don't post live location on social media** where strangers can see.
6. **Make sure location permission on your phone** is given only to apps that need it.

Sharing location with a partner or family is an agreement, not a right. Talk about it; don't set it up in secret.

## Save Battery and Data

Navigation drains the battery. A few ways to cut it: lower the screen brightness, use power-saving mode, carry a portable charger on long trips, and use offline maps so you aren't using data constantly. A phone holder in the vehicle helps, and remember not to stare at the screen while driving. Set the destination before you go, not while moving.

## When the Map Doesn't Match Reality

Maps can be wrong: new roads not yet recorded, places moved or closed. You can help fix it by reporting errors or sending updates through the features available. Business owners should also make sure their shop's information is accurate, such as opening hours, address, and phone number. Correct information means fewer lost customers.

Before your next trip, open offline maps and download one destination area. Two minutes now spares you the grey circle on an unfamiliar road later.

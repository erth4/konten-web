# How to Check for Hoaxes Before Sharing Information: Practical Steps You Can Try

A message lands in the family group chat: a warning about dangerous food, aid that is "only valid today", or shocking news about a public figure. Within seconds, a thumb has already hit forward. That is how misinformation spreads. Not because the sharer means harm, but because news that stirs emotion feels urgent to pass along. Fortunately, basic checks take only a few minutes.

## Why Hoaxes Are Easy to Believe

False information is usually built to grab attention. Common traits include panicked or angry headlines, calls to spread it immediately, claims from an unnamed "inside source", and packaging that mimics a news outlet or an official announcement. We also tend to trust messages sent by people close to us and news that fits our own beliefs. Recognizing these tendencies helps you pause before reacting.

## Five Quick Questions Before You Share

1. **Who is saying it?** Look for the author, outlet, or organization. A message with no clear source, such as "from a friend who works there", cannot be held accountable.
2. **What is the evidence?** Important claims need data, documents, or statements that can be traced. Be wary of vague phrases like "experts say" with no names.
3. **When was it published?** Old stories are often recirculated as if they just happened. Check both the publication date and the date of the event.
4. **Does anyone else report the same thing?** Big news is normally covered by several credible outlets. If only one account is spreading it, be suspicious.
5. **What am I being asked to do?** A push to forward immediately, fill in personal data, or click a link is a red flag, especially when it promises prizes or aid.

## Checking Images and Video

Photos are often used outside their original context. A picture of unrest from last year can be passed off as today's event. A few ways to check:

- Use **reverse image search** in a search engine to see where the image first appeared.
- Look for **odd details**: unreadable text, extra fingers, inconsistent shadows, or warped backgrounds. AI-generated and edited images often have flaws like these.
- For video, find **key frames** and trace the original source. A clipped video can change the meaning entirely.
- Be careful with voices or faces that feel unnatural, since digital imitations are getting easier to make.

## Check Links and Websites

Scammers often build sites that resemble news outlets or official institutions. Look closely at the domain: deliberately lookalike letters, strange endings, or names nearly identical to the real site. For government service information, type the official address yourself or use the official app rather than trusting links in chain messages.

## Use Fact-Checking Resources

Many popular claims have already been investigated by fact-checking organizations or official clarification channels. Search for the core sentence of the message together with "hoax" or "fact check". If a result exists, read the reasoning, not just the label. If there is no result, do not assume the claim is true; it means it has not been checked or there is not enough evidence yet.

## When in Doubt, Do Not Share

The most useful rule: if you are not sure, hold off. Sharing wrong information can cause panic, financial loss, or damage to someone's reputation, and it is hard to pull back once it spreads. Nobody requires you to be the first to deliver the news.

## If You Have Already Shared It

Delete the message or post, then send a short correction to the people who received it, for example "The information I sent earlier turned out to be wrong, please ignore it". A calm correction beats silence, and it shows that double-checking is normal.

## Closing

You do not need to be an expert to slow the spread of hoaxes. Just build a short pause: check the source, the date, and the evidence, then compare with other sources. Apply the five questions above to the next message that makes you want to forward it right away. This small habit protects you and the people around you.
